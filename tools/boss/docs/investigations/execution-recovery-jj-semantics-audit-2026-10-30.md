# Execution recovery audit: what it is for, what breaks, and what jj already provides

- Date: 2026-10-30
- Provenance: audit requested by the repo owner after a week of recovery-related dispatch failures. Investigation only; no code changes.
- Scope: the per-execution bookmarks `boss-base/*`, `boss-recovery/*` and `boss/*`; preserved-work containment; pointer reconciliation and adoption; pruning; cube's `pr/<n>` alias and its repair; the review pre-start checks that depend on all of these.
- Evidence: the recovery crate and its engine callers at `main` `f57cd2ee` (#3416); cube's bookmark code at the same revision; the shared mono store's jj operation log and git ref list (read-only); cube's audit logs `audit-2026-10-19.log` and `audit-2026-10-26.log`; GitHub history for 22 recovery PRs; jj 0.26.0 experiments in scratch repositories (0.26.0 is the jj installed on this host).
- Not accessed: the engine database and its audit log, which are engine-only. Counts of blocked items and attention items therefore come from the recovery PRs' own bodies, the owner's incident notes and the shared store, not from the engine.
- Related reading: [execution-recovery-pointers.md](../execution-recovery-pointers.md) (the design as documented), [revision-pr-anchor-recovery.md](revision-pr-anchor-recovery.md) (the `pr/<n>` alias incident), [cube-lease-purpose-and-boss-simplification.md](cube-lease-purpose-and-boss-simplification.md) (the lease-side simplification; this document is its recovery-side counterpart), #3405 (in review: archive and start fresh), #3320 (open: reviews stop inheriting predecessors).
- Citation convention: paths are repo-relative. `recovery/` means `tools/boss/engine/recovery/src/`, `core/` means `tools/boss/engine/core/src/`, `cube/` means `tools/cube/src/app/`.

## Verdict

The hypothesis is confirmed, with one qualification that shapes the proposal.

**Confirmed.** Recovery identifies work by commit id, decides containment by commit-id ancestry, and treats the two things jj does routinely when several workspaces share one store (merging concurrent operations into conflicted bookmarks, and rewriting commits so that old ids become hidden) as corruption that must stop an execution from starting. Each layer was added to defend the previous one against a failure the design itself produces: the baseline bookmark guards the diff floor; the publication head guards against cube's sweep of the recovery head; the recorded `base_commit` guards against the baseline bookmark being deleted; reconciliation guards against the lineage check; the `pr/<n>` alias and its repair guard reconciliation; the prune sweep and `cube repo gc` guard the store against the cost of all of the above; and three re-arm migrations guard the items the earlier layers wedged. Twenty-two PRs and roughly 34,000 added lines since 2026-09-15, eleven of them explicitly fixing the one before.

**Qualification.** jj does not keep everything forever in this deployment. cube abandons operations older than 72 hours and runs `jj util gc`, and a hidden commit cannot be addressed by change id at all, only by commit id, and only while an operation still references it. So one durable reference to a worker's unpushed tip is genuinely needed when that work must outlive three days. That is the single property worth keeping. Everything else is either something jj already provides (bookmarks follow rewrites, change ids survive rebases, `jj evolog` records predecessors, the operation log merges concurrent writers, a non-empty working-copy commit stays a visible head after the workspace moves on) or something that exists only to police the bookkeeping.

**The smallest design** creates nothing at dispatch. At teardown, if and only if the worker left unpublished non-empty commits, the engine records the tip's change id and commit id on the execution row and creates one `preserved/<execution>` bookmark on it. At the next dispatch it restages from that bookmark if the bookmark resolves to one commit, and otherwise leaves it where it is, raises an attention item and starts from the PR head. No baseline, no lineage check, no reconciliation, no containment proof, no `pr/<n>` alias, no vouchers, no re-arm migrations. Section 5 gives the migration as seven independently reviewable PRs, each deleting more than it adds.

Four root causes cover the table:

- **Identity by commit id** (F1, F2, F5): any rewrite below a pointer, including rewrites the engine performs itself, hides the ids the engine recorded.
- **Inconsistency is fatal** (F1, F2, F5, F6): a conflicted bookmark, a head that does not descend from the baseline, or a change the anchor cannot vouch for stops the execution instead of starting it fresh.
- **Single-writer assumption on a multi-writer store** (F3, F4, F5, F6, F10): the engine, cube, every worker and every health scan write one operation log and one `packed-refs`; jj merges those writes correctly, but the engine and cube then read the merge result as damage.
- **Per-execution references live in the view and in git** (F7, F8, F9): the cost is paid by every jj command on the host, in every workspace, for every execution ever run until pruned.

## 3. Value versus cost

### What can be measured about value

The engine database is not readable from a worker, so the number of executions that were resumed with real unpushed work is not available here. Three proxies are:

- cube's release audit marks a lease `preserved_unpushed` when the workspace head carries commits not reachable from any remote bookmark. Across all repositories, 10-19 to 10-30: 76 such releases, about 6 a day, against 380 to 1,616 leases a day. That is the upper bound on runs that ended with something to recover.
- `restore_for_retry` restages only when `unpublished_diff` is non-empty (`execution_bookmark.rs:738-740`); `restore_rebased` always restages. In the last 20 hours the store recorded 14 `duplicate` operations (one per restaging) against 174 executions dispatched.
- 559 visible `Resume recovered execution work` commits since 10-16, about 40 a day, almost all empty. Most are revision and review positioning, not recovered edits.

So recovery acts on a few percent of dispatches and carries real unpushed work on fewer still. The owner's own incident notes record no case in the last two weeks where the pointers recovered work that would otherwise have been lost, and two cases (F2, F5) where recovering the work by hand was faster than the machinery.

## 4. jj-semantics review

### What jj guarantees, checked on jj 0.26.0

Five experiments in a scratch repository with two workspaces on one store (the shape cube uses). Each line is observed output, not documentation.

1. **Rewrite.** `jj describe` on a bookmarked commit: the bookmark follows the rewrite; the old commit id is hidden but still addressable (`jj log -r <old id>` prints `hidden=true`); `jj evolog -r <change id>` lists both commits; the revset `(<old commit id>)::head & head` is empty while `(<change id>)::head & head` is the head. The engine's `descends` is the first form.
2. **Concurrent rewrite.** `jj describe` in one workspace and `jj rebase` in the other, both from the same operation: jj records "reconcile divergent operations", the change becomes divergent with two visible variants, every bookmark on it becomes conflicted with both variants as `+` sides and the old commit as the `-` side. `jj log -r 'bookmarks(exact:"name")'` prints two commits: exactly the "found 2" of F5. Resolution is one `jj bookmark set` and one `jj abandon`.
3. **Duplicate.** `jj duplicate <range> --insert-before @` (the restaging command at `redispatch.rs:112`) assigns a new change id to every copy; `jj interdiff` between original and copy is empty; `jj evolog` of the copy does list the original as a predecessor. `jj rebase -s` keeps the change id and also records the predecessor.
4. **Abandon.** `jj abandon` of a bookmarked commit prints "Deleted bookmarks: mark" in 0.26.0, and afterwards neither `jj log` nor `jj evolog` can address the change by change id ("Revision doesn't exist"); the commit id still works. This is the `MissingBaseline` cause the design document describes, and it is also why a recorded commit id is the right durable identity for a hidden commit.
5. **Concurrent bookmark moves** to related targets do not conflict: jj resolves a bookmark whose two new targets are ancestor and descendant to the descendant. Only unrelated targets (siblings, divergent variants) conflict.

Two facts about this deployment qualify "jj keeps everything":

- The store is colocated (`.jj/repo/store/git_target` is `../../../.git`), so every jj command imports git refs on entry and exports changed bookmarks on exit. Every bookmark the engine creates is also a git ref, every `jj git fetch` prunes the remote twins of merged per-execution branches, and all of that contends for one `packed-refs` lock (F7).
- `cube repo gc` abandons operations older than 72 hours and runs `jj util gc` (`cube/store_gc.rs:101`, `:633-699`). A hidden commit that no surviving operation references is collectable after that. A visible head is never collected; a non-empty working-copy commit stays a visible head after cube's `jj new main@origin` moves the workspace off it.

### Single-writer assumptions, listed

- The engine runs `jj bookmark set ... --allow-backwards` and `jj rebase` directly in the shared store (`-R <repo>`, `--ignore-working-copy`) while workers run jj in their workspaces and cube runs lease resets; nothing serialises them (`tools/cube/src/command_runner.rs:119-126` excludes the engine's and workers' jj from the maintenance gate; cube's `cube-pr-bookmarks.lock` covers only cube's alias writes).
- The conflict ladder's rung-1 rebase rewrites the PR's commits in the shared operation log while a revision worker may be live on the same PR (F5).
- Pointer snapshot and restore assume nothing else moved the pointers between the two reads.
- `recovery_predecessor` assumes a failed attempt leaves a trace it can skip.

## 5. Simplification proposal

### The property to keep

Unpushed worker edits are never silently destroyed. Everything below is measured against that and against the owner's rule that a recovery failure never stops work from starting.

### The design

1. **Dispatch creates nothing in the store.** No `boss-base/`, no `boss-recovery/`, no `boss/` at dispatch. The execution row records the PR head commit at dispatch (already `pr_head_before`) and, for a fresh item, `trunk()`'s commit. The worker's prompt loses the bookmark-advancing instructions. The push branch `boss/<exec>` is created by the worker's own `jj bookmark set boss/<exec> -r @` immediately before `cube pr create`; after merge GitHub deletes it, `jj git fetch` prunes the remote twin and cube's existing sweep forgets the local one.

2. **Teardown preserves, once, only when there is something to preserve.** When a run ends for any reason (completed, failed, blocked, orphaned, cancelled), the engine reads the workspace tip once: `@` of the workspace if it still exists, else the last `working_copies()` commit the journal recorded. It evaluates `(::tip ~ ::remote_bookmarks()) ~ empty()` in the store. If that set is empty, it records nothing. If it is non-empty, it records `(tip change id, tip commit id, count)` on the execution row and runs one operation: `jj bookmark create preserved/<exec> -r <tip>`. The bookmark keeps the commits alive across `cube repo gc`; the change id lets a later reader follow rewrites; the commit id addresses it even when hidden. `confirm_workspace` already does the read; `release_heads` and the orphan sweeps already have the hooks.

3. **Dispatch restages or starts fresh, never blocks.** For an implementation execution whose predecessor row carries a preserved tip:
   - Resolve `bookmarks(exact:"preserved/<pred>")`. One commit: `jj new <pr head or trunk>` in the new lease, then `jj rebase -s <preserved tip's unpublished roots> -d @` (keeping change ids; conflicts are left for the worker to resolve, as the restore report does today). Zero commits: nothing to restage. Two or more commits (conflicted): pick the variant that is an ancestor of the PR head if any, else the variant with the latest operation in `jj evolog`; if neither is decidable, leave the bookmark alone, raise one attention item naming it and start fresh.
   - An integrity verdict at this step (conflicted, divergent, unresolvable) is logged, raises the attention item, and dispatch continues from the PR head. A transport failure (ssh, gh, cube unavailable) retries as a positioning failure does today; it does not archive. The preserved bookmark is never moved or deleted by this step, so a human can still `jj log -r preserved/<exec>`.
   - Reviews and review guides never enter this path. They position on the PR head and start.
   - After the new execution publishes (its push succeeds), the predecessor's `preserved/*` is deleted in one operation. A preserved bookmark whose chain root is closed is deleted by the existing bookmark sweep after a fixed age; there is no proof to compute because the bookmark's commits were either restaged into a later execution (which pushed them) or the item was abandoned, and the attention item already told the operator which.

4. **The engine stops reading `pr/<n>`.** The PR head it needs is `tasks.pr_head_sha` (merge poller) or `<branch>@origin` after the fetch cube already performs on `goto --pr`. cube may keep the alias for human use; nothing in the engine names it.

5. **The engine stops rewriting PR history under a live worker.** Rung-1 and speculative rebases are deferred while a revision execution on the chain is live, the same way the chain hold already serialises writers; no pointer snapshots or restores exist to be raced.

6. **Conflicted or divergent state is resolved, not refused.** The one remaining reader (step 3) picks a variant or gives up loudly. cube's health scan stops iterating engine bookmarks because there are none to skip. Host pinning goes: a preserved tip is fetched to whichever host the dispatch chose, or archived there with the attention item if it cannot be.

What this leaves per execution in the store: zero bookmarks in the common case, one `preserved/*` for the few runs that end with unpushed work, and the PR branch the worker pushes, which GitHub needs anyway.

### What to delete and what to keep

Delete: `boss-base/*` and `boss-recovery/*` and everything that reads them (`resolve_pointers`, `resolve_base`, `evolved_baseline`, `durable_baseline`, `heal_baseline`, `head_bookmark_of`, `diff`, `unpublished_diff` as written); `reconcile.rs` and `adopt_pushed_head`; `snapshot.rs` and every snapshot-and-restore call around cube checkouts and engine rebases, including the host-execution journal's pointer snapshot and `snapshot_rebase_pointers`, `restore_rebase_pointers`, `finish_rebase_pointers`; the `pr_anchor` recognisers, `prepare_recovery_pr` and the engine's `repair_pr_bookmark` call; `prune.rs`, the prune sweep and its vouchers, the `prune_*` columns and the `execution_bookmark_prune_unproven` attention item; the re-arm recognisers (the shipped migrations stay, as the migration rules require); the `PointerIntegrityKind` taxonomy and `block_unrecoverable`; the lease-release bookmark gate; cube's `repair-pr-bookmark` command, `pr_gc.rs`, the recorded-head store and the `pr/*` parts of `pr_bookmarks.rs`, once the engine no longer depends on the alias; the `boss*` skip list in `health.rs`.

Keep: the execution row as the record of where a tip is; restaging onto the current base (with `jj rebase` instead of `jj duplicate`); the restore report and its worker instructions; the attention item for "preserved work not restaged"; cube's `cube repo gc`; cube's sweep of merged `boss/*` branches; the lease investigation's ownership work, which this design depends on for "one live writer per PR".

### Risks and what they cost

- **A worker that is still writing after its lease ends.** The lease investigation's ownership work covers this; preservation at teardown reads the tip after the pane is confirmed gone, as `confirm_workspace` does now.
- **Two live writers on one PR** (the F5 shape). Ownership prevents it; if it happens anyway, the teardown read sees a divergent change and preserves whichever variant is `@`; the other variant is visible in `jj evolog` and in the attention item. Nothing is deleted.
- **Hidden commits older than 72 hours.** Only a `preserved/*` bookmark or a visible head keeps them. The design creates the bookmark at the one moment it matters (teardown with unpushed work) rather than at every dispatch.
- **Restaging conflicts.** Handled as today: listed in the restore report for the worker.
- **Operators who relied on `boss-recovery/*` to inspect a run.** `preserved/*` plus the recorded tip on the row give the same view; `jj log -r <commit id>` works on hidden commits.
